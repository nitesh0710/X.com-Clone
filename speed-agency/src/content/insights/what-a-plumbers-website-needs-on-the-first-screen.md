When someone's water heater dies or a pipe bursts, they don't read your website. They glance at it. On a phone, that glance covers whatever fits on the screen before they scroll, and it decides whether they call you or hit the back button and try the next plumber.

So the first screen of a plumber's website matters more than every other part of it put together. Here's what needs to be there, and what doesn't.

## What "the first screen" means

The first screen is everything a visitor sees before scrolling. On a typical phone, that's not much: roughly the top 600–700 pixels. Your logo, a headline, a line or two of text and a button or two. That's the space you have.

Most plumbing websites waste it. A huge photo of a smiling plumber, a slogan like "Quality You Can Trust," and the phone number in tiny print in the corner. Look at your own site on your phone right now. Can you call yourself with one tap, without scrolling? If not, you're losing calls.

## 1. A phone number you can tap

This is the most important thing on the page. The number should be large, obvious and tappable, so touching it starts a call. Not a picture of a number, not a number in the footer, and not a "Contact Us" button that leads to a form.

Put it in two places: in a call button near the top of the page, and in a strip or header that stays visible as people scroll. Someone deciding in a panic shouldn't have to hunt.

If you can't answer every call, that's fine. Say when you're available, and set up a missed-call text-back so people who can't reach you get a quick reply instead of calling the next plumber.

## 2. Whether you handle emergencies

The second question every urgent visitor has is "can they come now?" Answer it straight away.

If you offer 24/7 emergency service, say so in a banner at the top: "24/7 emergency plumbing. Call now." If you don't, say what you do offer: "Same-day service, Monday to Saturday." Being honest here matters. A customer who calls at 2am and gets voicemail after reading "24/7" won't call again, and may leave a review about it.

## 3. A headline that says what you do

Your headline should tell a stranger, in a few words, what you do and that it's for them. "Plumbing repairs and water heaters in Springfield" beats "Excellence in Every Drop" every time.

People need to know within a second that they've landed in the right place. Put the service and the area in plain words.

## 4. Where you work

"Do they come out to me?" is the third question. Answer it on the first screen with a short line: "Serving Springfield, Shelbyville and surrounding towns." You can put the full list of towns further down the page or on a service-area page.

This helps Google too. When your website and your Google Business Profile both say clearly where you work, Google is more confident showing you to people searching in those towns.

## 5. One sign that you're the real deal

Urgent customers can't check much, but they notice a few things quickly. On the first screen, include one or two of these:

- Licensed and insured (with your license number if your state issues one)
- Your Google rating, if it's genuinely good, linked to your real reviews
- A real photo of your van, your team or your work, not a stock photo

Pick what's true and strongest for you. Don't cram all of them in, and never invent any of them.

## 6. A second option for non-urgent visitors

Not everyone on your site has water on the floor. Some are planning a water heater replacement or want a quote for a bathroom remodel. For them, a second button like "Get a free quote" leads to a short form.

Keep the form short: name, phone, town, and a box for what they need. Every extra field loses people. You can ask the detailed questions when you call them back.

## What to leave off the first screen

Just as important is what doesn't belong up there:

- **Big sliders or carousels.** They load slowly, push your number down and nobody waits for slide three.
- **Autoplay videos.** Slow on mobile data, and annoying.
- **A long mission statement.** Save your story for the about page. Urgent customers don't read it.
- **Pop-ups.** Anything that covers the page and needs to be closed before someone can call you is costing you jobs.
- **Five different buttons.** Call, and get a quote. That's enough.

## Speed counts as part of the first screen

A first screen that takes eight seconds to appear isn't a first screen, it's a blank page. Plumbing customers are often on a phone with patchy signal, standing in a flooded basement. If your site is slow, they won't wait.

Big photos, heavy page builders and lots of plugins are the usual causes. A well-built site should show its first screen in a couple of seconds, even on a slow connection.

## A quick test you can do today

Open your website on your phone, not your computer. Then ask yourself:

1. Can I call with one tap without scrolling?
2. Does it say whether I can get help today?
3. Can I tell what this business does and where in under three seconds?
4. Is there a reason to trust them that I can see right away?
5. Did it load quickly?

If the answer to any of these is no, that's the first thing to fix, before any talk of SEO or advertising. Getting more people to your site doesn't help if the first screen sends them away.
