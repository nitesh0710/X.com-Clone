## How HVAC customers search

HVAC is the most seasonal of the trades, and the searches show it.

In summer, it's cooling: "AC repair", "AC not cooling", "AC blowing warm air", "central air installation". In winter, it flips to heating: "furnace repair", "no heat", "heat pump not working", "boiler repair". In spring and fall, people search for tune-ups and maintenance, and that's when planned replacements happen, because nobody wants to replace a system in the middle of a heat wave if they can avoid it.

The urgent searches are high stakes. A family with no AC in July, or no heat in January, calls the first company that looks trustworthy and can come soon. The planned searches are slower and more price-conscious: they compare companies, read about efficiency and financing, and ask for estimates.

## What an HVAC site needs

Here's how I'd build a site for your HVAC business.

**A seasonal banner and featured service.** The top of the homepage changes with the season: "AC not cooling? Same-week repairs" in summer, "No heat? Call now" in winter. It's a small change that makes the site feel current and matches what people are searching for right now.

**Separate cooling and heating sections.** Each has its own pages for repair, replacement and maintenance. Someone searching "heat pump repair" lands on a page about heat pumps, not a generic list.

**Tap-to-call on every screen.** For no-heat and no-cooling calls, the phone is the fastest path. The button stays in reach on every page.

**A quote request for replacements.** A new system is a big purchase. A short form (home size, current system type if known, what's going wrong) lets you prepare a better estimate.

**Maintenance plan page.** If you offer one, this page explains what's included and lets customers sign up. It's often the best way to keep your schedule full in spring and fall.

**Financing mention, if you offer it.** Many homeowners need to spread the cost of a new system. If you work with a financing provider, a clear line about it (and a link) removes a reason to hesitate. If you don't offer financing, the site won't suggest you do.

**Service area and trust details.** The towns you cover, your license and insurance, and any manufacturer or industry certifications you actually hold.

## Getting ready before the rush

The best time to launch an HVAC website is a few weeks before your busy season, so Google has time to find the new pages and your Business Profile is pointing at them when the searches spike. If you're reading this in the off-season, that's good timing.

I won't promise rankings or a number of calls. I'll build the site properly, connect it to your Google Business Profile and set up a way to count calls and quote requests, so you can compare one season to the next.
