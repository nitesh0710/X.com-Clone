## How electrical customers search

Electricians get a wider spread of jobs than most trades, and that changes the website.

At one end are small, often urgent jobs: an outlet that's stopped working, lights that flicker, a breaker that keeps tripping, a ceiling fan to hang. These customers search "electrician near me" or describe the problem, then call whoever looks reliable and available.

At the other end are planned projects: panel upgrades, EV charger installs, rewiring an older home, adding circuits for a remodel. These customers take their time. They compare a few electricians, look for licensing and insurance, and want an estimate before anyone comes out.

A single "Contact us" page serves neither group well. The repair customer wants a button they can tap. The project customer wants to explain the job and get a price.

## What an electrician's site needs

Here's how I'd build a site for your electrical business.

**Two clear paths from the homepage.** "Need a repair?" leads straight to tap-to-call. "Planning a bigger job?" leads to an estimate form. People pick their path in a second, and you get the right kind of enquiry.

**An estimate form that asks the useful questions.** For panel upgrades and EV chargers, a few extra questions (age of the home, current panel size if known, what they want to add) help you quote without a wasted trip. For repairs, the form stays short.

**License and insurance up front.** Electrical work is the trade people worry about most. Your license number, insurance and any certifications sit near the top of every page, not hidden in the footer.

**Service pages for the jobs you want more of.** Panel upgrades, EV chargers, lighting, rewiring, generator hookups. Each page explains the job, what affects the cost and how long it usually takes. These are the pages that match what people type into Google.

**A service-area page.** The towns you cover, listed plainly. Electricians often have a wider radius for big jobs than for small ones. If that's true for you, the page can say so.

**Real photos when you have them.** A clean panel install photographs well and shows care. Until you have photos, I'll launch with tidy placeholders and swap yours in later.

## Small jobs and big jobs on one site

The biggest mistake I see on electricians' sites is treating every visitor the same. Splitting the paths means the person with a tripped breaker isn't scrolling past EV charger copy, and the homeowner planning a panel upgrade sees that you take bigger projects seriously.

## What I won't promise

I won't promise a number of calls or a position on Google. I'll build the site properly, connect it to your Google Business Profile and set up a way to count calls and estimate requests, so you can judge it on real numbers.
