## How roofing customers search

Roofing searches are research-heavy and often triggered by weather.

After a hail storm or high winds, searches like "roof repair near me", "storm damage roof" and "roof inspection" jump. Some of those homeowners have a leak and need help this week. Many more just want to know if there's damage, and they'll take a free inspection from whoever seems most trustworthy.

Replacement searches are slower. A roof is one of the biggest repairs a homeowner will ever pay for, so they compare companies carefully. They look at photos of past jobs, read reviews, check whether you're licensed and insured, and look for any sign that you'll still be around if something goes wrong.

That makes a roofer's website different from a plumber's. The call button still matters, but proof of work matters more.

## What a roofer's site needs

Here's how I'd build a site for your roofing business.

**A job photo gallery.** This is the most important part of the site. Before-and-after photos of real jobs, grouped by type (repairs, full replacements, gutters), with a one-line caption each. I compress the images so the gallery stays fast on a phone.

**A free inspection offer.** For many homeowners, "book a free inspection" feels like a smaller step than "get a quote." The form asks for name, phone, address and what they've noticed, and it sits near the top of every page.

**A storm damage page.** A plain guide to what to do after a storm: what to look for, when to call, and how you handle it. It's genuinely useful, and it matches what people search for in the days after bad weather.

**Financing, if you offer it.** A clear mention on the homepage and the replacement page. If you don't offer financing, the site won't hint that you do.

**Trust details up front.** License, insurance, any manufacturer certifications you actually hold, and your warranty terms written in plain English.

**Tap-to-call and a service-area page.** For the leak-in-the-ceiling customer, the number is always one tap away. The service-area page lists the towns you cover.

**Reviews, once they're real.** When you have Google reviews, I'll feature a few and link to your profile. Until then, that section stays hidden. No placeholders, no made-up quotes.

## What I won't promise

I won't promise a flood of storm-season calls or a ranking position. Those depend on your area, your competitors and your reviews. I'll build the site properly for Google, connect it to your Business Profile, and set up a way to count calls and inspection requests so you can see what it's doing.
