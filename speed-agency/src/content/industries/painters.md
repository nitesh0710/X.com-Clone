## How painting customers search

Painting searches are driven by photos and estimates.

A homeowner planning to repaint the living room, the exterior or the kitchen cabinets usually isn't in a rush. They search "house painters near me", "interior painting" or "cabinet painting", then look through a few websites and Google profiles. What decides it is the work: do the photos look clean and professional, and does this look like a company that will turn up, protect the furniture and finish on time?

After that, it's the estimate. Most people get two or three before choosing. The easier you make it to ask for one, and the faster you reply, the more of those comparisons you'll be part of.

Commercial and property-manager work behaves differently. Those buyers want to see bigger projects, your crew size and your insurance, and they often send a request for a proposal rather than filling in a web form.

## What a painter's site needs

Here's how I'd build a site for your painting business.

**A gallery that leads.** Your best photos, grouped by type: interiors, exteriors, cabinets, commercial. Before-and-after pairs work especially well. The gallery is near the top of the homepage, not hidden on a separate page nobody clicks.

**A simple estimate form.** Name, phone, address, what needs painting and when they'd like it done. It lands in your email and as a text on your phone, so you can reply quickly.

**Service pages for the work you want more of.** Interior, exterior, cabinet refinishing, deck staining, commercial. Each page shows relevant photos, explains your process (prep, protection, cleanup) and offers the estimate form.

**Your process, written plainly.** Customers worry about mess, delays and surprise costs. A short "how a job works" section (estimate, scheduling, prep, painting, walkthrough) answers those worries before they're raised.

**Trust details.** Insurance, any license your state requires, the paint brands you use if you have a preference, and your warranty or touch-up policy.

**Tap-to-call and a service-area page.** Some people would rather just call. The number is always visible, and the towns you cover are listed plainly.

**Reviews when you have them.** Real Google reviews, featured and linked. Nothing invented in the meantime.
