## How plumbing customers actually search

Plumbing searches split into two groups, and your website has to serve both.

The first group is urgent. A water heater has failed, a toilet is overflowing, or there's water coming through the ceiling. These people search on their phone, often at night, and they decide in seconds. They don't read your about page. They look for a phone number, a sign that you cover their area, and some hint that you'll actually pick up.

The second group is planning. They want a quote for a new water heater, a bathroom repipe or a sewer line camera inspection. They have time to compare, so they look at a few sites, check reviews and fill in a form or two.

Most plumbing websites are built for neither. The number is small and hidden in the header, the services are one long list, and the contact form asks for ten fields.

## What a plumber's site needs

Here's how I'd build a site for your plumbing business.

**An emergency banner on every page.** A strip at the top with your number and a line like "Burst pipe? Call now." On a phone, tapping it starts the call. If you don't offer 24/7 service, the banner says your real hours instead, so nobody calls at 2am and feels misled.

**Tap-to-call that's always in reach.** A large call button on the first screen of every page, so it's visible without scrolling.

**A short quote form.** Name, phone, town and "what's going on?" That's it. The request lands in your email and as a text on your phone.

**One page per main service.** Water heaters, drain cleaning, leak repair, repiping, sewer lines. Each page explains the job in plain words, what affects the price, and how to book. These pages are what show up when someone searches for that exact job in your area.

**A service-area page.** A plain list of the towns and neighborhoods you cover. It answers "do you come out to me?" before anyone has to ask, and it helps Google understand where you work.

**Trust details near the top.** Your license number, whether you're insured, and how long you've been working, if you want to say it. Real photos of your van, your team or your work beat stock photos every time.

**A reviews section.** Once you have real Google reviews, I'll show a few and link to your profile. Until then the section stays hidden. I don't use placeholder reviews.

## What I won't do

I won't promise you'll rank first for "plumber near me." That depends on your area, how many competitors you have and your reviews. What I will do is build the site properly for Google and connect it to your Business Profile, then set up a way to count calls and form requests, so you can see what the site is doing.
